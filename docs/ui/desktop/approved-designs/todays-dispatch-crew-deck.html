Today's Dispatch — the Crew Deck

The scheduler engine's operator surface: one zone-scoped cockpit for the operating day, with three modes — Plan (what the next run will do; holds are the one pre-run lever; Run dispatch), Live (the engineer deck, critical interception, and the work rail), and Replay (a past run in the same layout, with the decision stream as its inspector). This is the automatic engine's face; the Assign Work Console (/assign, #272) remains the separate manual write surface — #280 R7 stands.

Provenance. Designed 2026-08-20 in the working artifact “Today's Dispatch — Scheduler UI” (claude.ai artifact 2d66798b-daa4-463f-9db0-351d8ce1195b) alongside the Assign Work Console; it is the “dispatch-board grammar” #272:64 refers to. The artifact was never committed, which led #280 (2026-08-24) to record — factually wrongly — that “no wireframe exists for this screen set.” Recovered from the artifact's embedded artboards and APPROVED 2026-08-25 (operator) — owning record #282. The two wireframes below are the authoritative part; the concept boards document the directions considered and why they lost.

What the boards encode

  • Header: zone · operating date · run-status pill (Dispatched 05:00 · Success) · Plan | Live | Replay mode toggle · Run dispatch · find ticket / SE.
  • Situation chips: placed · unassignable · held · critical-needs-you · over capacity · changes today. All live counts — never hard-coded.
  • Critical interception strip: the #258 Q-B escalation surfaced as a first-class interruption (“no capacity-eligible SE — escalated · ZM alerted — Assign manually”).
  • Engineer deck: one card per SE — tier badge, load used/cap, numbered plant groups holding ordered stop chips (persisted stop_sequence / sort_order; ordinal, deliberately no clock/ETA per #258 Q6), exception card states (overloaded · headroom “first candidate for rebalance” · on-leave with coverage note).
  • Provenance grammar (non-negotiable, shared with #272): solid border + dot = system decision · dashed + role·initials = human override · heavy crimson = critical direct-assigned, leads the route · RET = vehicle return-date priority · ghost = not on a route. A human decision never looks like a system one. The UI may only render provenance the data truthfully supports.
  • Work rail: Unassignable (itemised, with NO_COVERAGE / ALL_DROPPED reasons) · Held / deferred (return date + approver) · Withheld by policy (below-threshold) · Changes today (adds · removes · swaps, IST-day-bounded).
  • Override flow: inspect → understand (the persisted decision trace: coverage, availability, capacity-at-decision, kit, clustering, not-enforced filters) → override → preview impact before commit (both lanes' capacity, the run's own ranking of the target, route effect, conflicts) → confirm with a mandatory audited reason. A lost race re-presents as a clean conflict, never an error page (#265).

Concept exploration (context, not authoritative): A · Route Ribbons — B · Crew Deck — C · Decision Stream — D · Zone Map
A · Route Ribbons
Horizontal lane per SE: capacity bar at the left, then the day's route as an ordinal chain — stop after stop, connected. Rail of unplaced work below.
Unplaced
Strength: aligned lanes make SE-to-SE route comparison effortless; the journey metaphor is honest (ordinal, no fake clock).
Trade-off: reads as a decorated table at a glance; long rows scroll horizontally; small chips make weak drop targets.
B · Crew Deck
Recommended
One card per SE: capacity ring + ordered stop list. Exceptions dock at the side — problems live physically outside the crew.
9/8 over capacity
On leave
Not on a route
Why it wins: a whole zone fits one screen; capacity is a shape, not a column; every card is a big drop target; problems are visibly separated from the crew.
Trade-off: comparing two SEs' routes side-by-side is weaker than aligned lanes; long routes truncate inside a card.
C · Decision Stream
The engine's own processing order as a vertical ledger: one card per ticket decision — priority, arrow, chosen SE, why-chips. Unassignable outcomes appear in place.
Unassignable · no coverage
Override
Strength: unmatched explainability — the UI is the algorithm's own order; perfect for audit and replay; scales to any ticket count.
Trade-off: no workload gestalt — "who is overloaded?" is invisible; 200 cards is reading, not commanding. Best as the inspector inside another concept, not the primary surface.
D · Zone Map
Geographic dispatch: plant markers sized by open work, SE route polylines from home base through their stops. Spatially truthful for a field-service domain.
solid = system route · dashed = overridden leg · dashed marker = no coverage
Strength: highest wow; distance and clustering become literally visible; coverage holes are geographic facts.
Trade-off: honest blockers today — SE home coordinates are optional Phase-1 data, plant geometry coverage is unverified, no map stack exists in the app, and routes without times risk implying precision. Right as a later overlay, wrong as the primary surface.
